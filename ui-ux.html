💉

Task Analysis

Hierarchical Task Analysis (HTA)

Hierarchical decomposition of the system's core task, including specification plans describing the order and conditions of subtasks.

0. Log Meal & Dose Insulin
Plan 0: Do 1, 2 and 3 sequentially.
If CGM sync fails, do 2.1.
1. Determine Carbs
Plan 1: Do 1.1 then 1.2
1.1 Scan food with camera
1.2 Confirm AI estimation
2. Get Current Glucose
2.1 Input glucose manually
(Exception: CGM connection lost)
3. Administer Dose
Plan 3: Do 3.1 then 3.2
3.1 Review AI dosage
3.2 Confirm & log to system

Methodology: The HTA provides a formal specification plan outlining the order and conditions under which subtasks are performed. The starting point is the user's primary goal, cascading downwards until no new relevant elements can be inserted.

Conceptual Model Communication

Norman's Design Principles

How we successfully communicated the conceptual model to our users through careful UI design[cite: 109].

🔘

Affordances

Properties that determine how the object could be used[cite: 110].

In our App:

The primary action buttons (like 'Log Meal') have a 3D shadow and elevated appearance, clearly affording "pushing"[cite: 112].

🏷️

Signifiers

Signs that communicate where the action should take place[cite: 113].

In our App:

Unambiguous icons (a camera for scanning, a syringe for dosing) act as signifiers so users perceive the affordances clearly[cite: 113].

🔄

Mapping

The relationship between the controls and their effects on the system[cite: 114].

In our App:

Natural mapping[cite: 114, 115]: increasing the carbohydrate slider visually raises the insulin dose bar directly above it.

⚡

Feedback

Communicating the results of an action immediately to avoid false causality[cite: 116].

In our App:

Immediate visible feedback[cite: 116]: clicking 'Save' triggers a synchronized success animation and vibration (haptic feedback)[cite: 117].

🔒

Constraints

Restricting the number of possibilities to reduce alternatives and errors[cite: 118].

In our App:

Logical constraints[cite: 118]: the 'Confirm Dose' button is physically grayed out (disabled) until a blood glucose value is received.

Usability Standards

Nielsen's 10 Heuristics Applied

Key examples of how SmartGlycoAI complies with established usability heuristics to ensure a seamless experience.

1

Visibility of System Status

The user's current blood glucose level and recent trend graph are prominently displayed on the Home screen at all times. There is never a doubt about current health status.

3

User Control and Freedom

Users can open past meals in the Diary and edit carbohydrate entries or delete incorrect logs (as demonstrated in Scenario 2), providing an "emergency exit".

4

Consistency and Standards

Medical color coding (Green for safe, Red for alert) and standard iconography (camera icon for photos, graph icon for trends) align with existing medical app conventions.

5

Error Prevention

Before an insulin dose is logged to the system, a confirmation modal requires explicit user approval, preventing accidental injections or logging errors.

HCI Theory

Usability Trade-offs: Novices vs. Experts

It is not always possible to achieve optimal scores for all usability attributes simultaneously. Here is how we balanced Learnability and Efficiency.

The Novice Profile (Needs Learnability)

A recently diagnosed patient (ignorant about the domain) interacting with the system for the first time (minimal experience). To lower the cognitive barrier, SmartGlycoAI prioritizes Learnability by offering the AI Food Scanner as the primary path, hiding complex mathematical inputs.

The Expert Profile (Needs Efficiency)

A veteran diabetic (knowledgeable about the domain) who uses the app daily (extensive experience). Taking a photo every time becomes a bottleneck. For them, we prioritize Efficiency by providing a quick manual-entry numpad, bypassing the AI entirely.

*Based on Jakob Nielsen's Usability Engineering (1993) model of user experience progression.

Accessibility (A11y)

Designing for healthcare requires strict adherence to accessibility standards to ensure safety during cognitive overload or physical distress (e.g., hypoglycemia).

  • ✓

    Touch Targets (48x48px Minimum)

    All critical action buttons (like insulin registration) are sized according to iOS/Android guidelines to prevent accidental mis-taps.

  • ✓

    Color Blindness Independence

    System status is never communicated by color alone. Alerts use distinct iconography (e.g., warning triangles) alongside text labels.

  • ✓

    High Contrast Ratios (WCAG AA)

    Text regarding blood glucose levels maintains a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1 against backgrounds for readability under glare.

🛡️

Error Prevention & Edge Cases

In a healthcare application, the "happy path" is not enough. The interface must be resilient to hardware failures, network loss, and AI uncertainties to guarantee patient safety at all times.

📶

No Internet Connection

The user is in a basement or area with no cellular data during a meal.

UI Fallback Offline-First: Logs are saved to a local SQLite/Hive database and core math algorithms run locally. Syncs to cloud once reconnected.
🤖

Low AI Confidence

The camera scans a complex meal (e.g., stew) and the Computer Vision model is unsure.

UI Fallback Manual Override: The UI displays a "Low Confidence" badge and automatically expands a numeric keypad for manual carbohydrate entry.
⌚

CGM Sensor Lost

Bluetooth connection to the FreeStyle Libre drops right before a calculation.

UI Fallback Graceful Degradation: A highly visible red banner appears at the top. The dose calculator switches from "Automatic" to requesting a capillary blood drop input.
