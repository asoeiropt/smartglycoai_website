Application & Tools

About SmartGlycoAI

SmartGlycoAI is an intelligent Type 1 Diabetes management application designed to relieve the cognitive burden of calculating carbohydrates and insulin doses. It features an AI-driven food scanner, predictive glucose alerts, and a clinical dashboard for endocrinologists.

Development Stack

  • 📱 Frontend Framework: Flutter (Dart) for high-fidelity cross-platform UI.
  • 🎨 Design Tool: Figma (for initial mockups and asset generation).
  • 💻 IDE: Visual Studio Code & Android Studio.

Briefing & Scenarios

The following briefing and scenarios (developed in Stage 3) will help evaluators understand and test the application.

📋 User Briefing

"Welcome and thank you for participating! Managing Type 1 Diabetes requires constantly balancing food intake, physical activity, and insulin. SmartGlycoAI is an application designed to relieve this burden. It estimates the carbohydrates in your meals, tracks your glucose via a sensor, and suggests the correct insulin dose so you don't have to do the math manually. During this test, we want you to interact with the interface naturally to complete a few tasks. Please 'think aloud' as you go, so we understand your thought process. Remember, we are testing the interface, not you!"

Scenario 1

Lunch at a restaurant

João Antunes is a young adult with type 1 diabetes who uses a FreeStyle Libre sensor. On Tuesday at 1 p.m., he is having lunch with his friends at a restaurant and orders steak with rice and salad. When the food arrives, his glucose is 115 mg/dL and stable. He needs to figure out the carbohydrates in his meal, which he suspects has more rice than usual (around 60g instead of an initial guess of 45g). Using the system, his goal is to register this meal discreetly, adjust the carbohydrate estimation, and receive the correct insulin dose recommendation before he starts eating, so his friends barely notice.

Scenario 2

A low on the way home

At 6 p.m., João is on the bus home after the gym. He receives a predictive alert indicating his glucose will reach 65 mg/dL in 20 minutes and suggesting he consume 15 g of fast-acting carbohydrates. He drinks a small pack of juice. He must use the system to log that he took this corrective action. Later that evening, at home, he reviews his day. He remembers that at lunch he left part of the rice on the plate, meaning he ate 45 g instead of the 60 g he previously recorded. His goal now is to use the system to locate his past lunch entry and correct the carbohydrate amount, ensuring his daily logs are accurate.

Scenario 3

Preparing an appointment

Dr. Joana Silvia is an endocrinologist at a hospital in Almada, and João has an appointment with her on Thursday. Before the consultation, she uses the clinical system on her office computer to review João's data from the past 30 days. She needs to identify any problematic patterns, such as the late afternoon lows on gym days and high glucose after lunch. During the appointment, they discuss the data and decide to change his insulin-to-carb ratio from 1 unit per 15 g to 1 unit per 12 g. João must then use the system to update this clinical setting.

Scope & Limitations

The prototype has a "low fidelity in depth", meaning the backend is mocked to support the main test scenarios. Below is what evaluators should expect:

✅ What is Working

  • Complete navigation flow between Home, Camera, and Diary.
  • Interaction with the Meal Logging screen (Scenario 1).
  • Responding to the predictive low-glucose alert pop-up (Scenario 2).
  • Editing past entries in the Diary tab (Scenario 2).
  • Profile settings updates (Scenario 3).

🚧 Incomplete Parts (Mocked)

  • Computer Vision: The camera opens, but the AI recognition is hardcoded to "steak with rice and salad".
  • Bluetooth CGM: The glucose values are static and not pulled from a real sensor.
  • Backend Database: Data resets when the application is fully closed.
