Testing Preparation

📋 User Briefing (Script read to participants)

"Welcome and thank you for participating! Managing Type 1 Diabetes requires constantly balancing food intake, physical activity, and insulin. SmartGlycoAI is an application designed to relieve this burden. It estimates the carbohydrates in your meals, tracks your glucose via a sensor, and suggests the correct insulin dose so you don't have to do the math manually. During this test, we want you to interact with the interface naturally to complete a few tasks. Please 'think aloud' as you go, so we understand your thought process. Remember, we are testing the interface, not you!"

Evaluation Roles

Roles were swapped after every test so that each group member played each role.

The 3 Scenarios (From Stage 2)

View Stage 2 Task Analysis →
Scenario 1 • Patient Task 1

Meal Time at a Restaurant

João is at a restaurant and is unsure about the carbohydrate count of his pasta dish. Glancing at his Home screen, he views his stable 6-hour glucose trend graph. He opens SmartGlycoAI's meal logging module, takes a photo of his plate, and the app estimates 65g of carbs. Based on his current CGM reading of 110 mg/dL, the app suggests a precise insulin dose before he starts eating.

User Sheet: You are having dinner at a restaurant and have just been served a plate of pasta. Before you start eating, use the app to find out how much insulin you should take, and save the meal.
Scenario 2 • Patient Tasks 3 & 4

A low on the way home

At 6 p.m. João is on the bus home after the gym. His phone vibrates with an alert: the app predicts his glucose will reach 65 mg/dL in 20 minutes and suggests eating 15 g of fast-acting carbohydrates. He opens the alert, drinks a small pack of juice he always carries, and taps "Register correction". Later that evening, at home, he opens the Diary to check his day. He remembers that at lunch he left part of the rice on the plate, so he ate fewer carbohydrates than the 60 g he registered. He opens the lunch entry, changes the carbohydrates to 45 g, keeps the 4.0 units he actually took, and saves.

User Sheet: You are on the bus after the gym and your phone warns you that your glucose is dropping. Deal with the warning. Later, at home, you remember that at lunch you only ate 45 g of carbohydrates instead of the 60 g you registered: correct this in the app.
Scenario 3 • Doctor Task 2

Routine Clinical Check-up

Dr. Joana is preparing for a consultation with João. She opens the platform's professional dashboard to review his data from the past month. She notices frequent hypoglycemic events during the night and decides to adjust his baseline insulin ratio directly within the app's professional settings.

User Sheet: You are João's endocrinologist and he has an appointment with you tomorrow. Find out how often his glucose went too low during the last month, and change his insulin-to-carbohydrate ratio to 1 unit per 12 g.

Evaluation Observations

Users generally understood the purpose of the app from the briefing and were able to start each scenario on their own. However, usability tests and expert feedback revealed some hesitation during navigation and highlighted areas where medical terminology and tracking could be more precise.

Usability Problem Found Proposed Solution
Issue 1: Users hesitated when looking for the diary edit button. It was not clear that tapping the entry would open the edit mode. Add a visible pencil icon (✏️) next to each diary entry so the edit action is immediately recognizable.
Issue 2: During the alert scenario, users dismissed the notification but didn't know where to find the suggested carb correction afterwards. Keep a persistent "Active Alert" banner at the top of the Home screen until the user logs the correction.
Issue 3: The interface used the generic word "Dose", which was considered too ambiguous for a medical context. Rename the label specifically to "Insulin" or "Insulin Dose" to prevent any misunderstandings.
Issue 4: The system suggests an insulin dose, but there is no explicit way to confirm if the patient actually administered it. Add a confirmation checkbox for the user to explicitly validate that they took the suggested insulin dose.
Issue 5: When reviewing historical data, it was difficult to quickly identify anomalous events or days where the patient felt unwell. Use the calendar days to highlight when the patient was unwell, providing contextual clinical info or notes when selecting those specific dates.
